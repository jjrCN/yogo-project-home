Why YOGO

Existing 3DGS pipelines often grow unpredictably, benchmark too easily on sparse viewpoints, and break under messy multi-sensor reality. YOGO treats memory budget, fusion robustness, and evaluation difficulty as first-class design constraints.

Deterministic budget control Availability-registration fusion Immersion v1.0 benchmark

YOGO reframes 3D Gaussian Splatting as a controllable system rather than an uncontrolled growth process.

Abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting has transformed neural rendering, yet many existing methods remain misaligned with real production constraints. YOGO reformulates stochastic Gaussian growth into a deterministic, budget-aware equilibrium, introduces a new availability-registration protocol for robust multi-sensor fusion, and presents Immersion v1.0, an ultra-dense indoor benchmark designed to break the "sparsity shield." Across extensive experiments, YOGO achieves state-of-the-art visual quality while maintaining a strictly predictable resource profile.

Overview

YOGO is built to close the gap between research-grade 3DGS demos and production-grade reconstruction systems.

Controllable compute

Instead of heuristic growth, YOGO lets users target explicit point budgets and reason about the fidelity-memory trade-off ahead of time.

Robust sensor fusion

A dynamic availability-registration protocol filters radiometric inconsistency and sensor pollution before fusion corrupts the optimization process.

Harder evaluation

Immersion v1.0 saturates viewpoint coverage so methods are tested on physical fidelity, not rescued by benchmark sparsity.

Method at a Glance

The framework is organized around four core contributions described in the paper.

01

Deterministic Budget Control

A feedback controller replaces uncontrolled densification and enforces hardware-defined primitive budgets, with the option to localize fidelity in high-value regions.

02

Availability-Registration Fusion

A dynamic protocol quantifies data availability and filters inconsistent multi-modal inputs before they destabilize the reconstruction pipeline.

03

Solid Optimization Suite

Area-normalized gradient accumulation, maximum effective opacity pruning, and principal-axis densification preserve detail while compressing model scale.

04

Immersion v1.0 Benchmark

An ultra-dense indoor benchmark with saturated multi-sensor acquisition shifts evaluation away from sparse-view synthesis toward absolute physical fidelity.

Immersion v1.0 Benchmark

The benchmark is deliberately designed to remove the sparsity safety net.

Typical indoor datasets provide a few hundred to a few thousand frames per scene. In contrast, Immersion v1.0 uses a six-loop saturated acquisition protocol to produce over 30,000 synchronized multi-sensor frames per scene, with roughly 15x more initial geometry than ScanNet++ and substantially denser viewpoint coverage.

This pushes methods beyond interpolation comfort zones and exposes whether they can truly preserve structure, detail, and physical consistency under dense observation.

Key Experimental Takeaways

Predictable budget-quality trade-offs

YOGO makes the performance-memory frontier explicit instead of leaving model size to opaque densification heuristics.

Clear saturation behavior

On the reported SSS track, moving from YOGO1 to YOGO2 yields a notable Qalign gain, while the jump to YOGO3 brings only marginal improvement.

Efficiency over raw point count

The constrained YOGO1 setting at 1.5M points already surpasses AbsGS at 4.28M points in the paper's reported rendering comparison.

Better stress-testing for baselines

Methods that rely on expensive global statistics or extensive recalibration hit severe overhead and OOM issues under Immersion v1.0's density.
