Overview

Built to close the industry-academia gap in 3DGS

The paper identifies three practical blockers that keep many 3D Gaussian Splatting systems in the research-prototype stage: uncontrolled model growth, benchmarks that reward hallucination over physical fidelity, and severe pollution when fusing heterogeneous sensor inputs.

YOGO responds with a system-level redesign. Instead of letting heuristics decide model size, it enforces a hard budget. Instead of evaluating on sparsity-protected datasets, it introduces Immersion v1.0, an indoor benchmark with near-continuous observation. Instead of naive sensor fusion, it filters data by availability and radiometric consistency before training.

What changes

A controllable 3DGS pipeline that treats memory and fidelity as first-class design constraints.

Why it matters

Teams can choose a budget target up front, reproduce it reliably, and still retain strong rendering quality.

What the benchmark tests

Whether a method can model real structure under dense, multi-sensor, physically demanding capture conditions.

Abstract

A concise paper summary for visitors landing on the page

3D Gaussian Splatting has transformed neural rendering, but many existing methods remain poorly aligned with real production constraints. YOGO reformulates stochastic Gaussian growth into a deterministic, budget-aware equilibrium, adds a new availability-registration protocol for robust multi-sensor fusion, and introduces Immersion v1.0, an ultra-dense indoor benchmark designed to break the "sparsity shield." Across extensive experiments, YOGO delivers state-of-the-art visual quality while preserving a strictly predictable resource profile.

Core Design

Four contributions that define the YOGO framework

01

Deterministic Budget Control

Heuristic growth is replaced with a feedback controller that enforces hardware-defined primitive budgets and can localize fidelity to spatial regions of interest.

02

Availability-Registration Fusion

A dynamic multi-sensor filtering protocol explicitly models data availability, radiometric inconsistency, and noisy measurements before fusion.

03

Solid Optimization Suite

Targeted components such as area-normalized gradient accumulation, maximum effective opacity pruning, and principal-axis densification sharpen structure while compressing model scale.

04

Immersion v1.0 Benchmark

An ultra-dense indoor benchmark with saturated multi-sensor acquisition that shifts evaluation from sparse-view interpolation toward absolute physical fidelity.

Benchmark

Immersion v1.0 is designed to remove the sparsity safety net

Typical indoor benchmarks offer hundreds of images per scene, with ScanNet++ averaging around 1,900. Immersion v1.0 pushes well beyond that regime with a six-loop saturated acquisition protocol producing more than 30,000 synchronized multi-sensor frames per scene.

The dataset also reports a much denser geometric substrate: roughly 2.55 million initial points per scene, about 15x larger than the approximately 161K initial points reported for ScanNet++.

Results

Key takeaways you can surface before adding full figures and videos

Predictable trade-offs

YOGO exposes the performance-memory curve explicitly instead of hiding it behind uncontrolled densification.

Clear saturation point

On the SSS track, moving from YOGO1 to YOGO2 with about 2M extra points yields a notable +0.13 Qalign gain, while another 2M points to YOGO3 adds only 0.04 more.

Efficiency over raw scale

Even the constrained YOGO1 setting at 1.5M points surpasses the rendering quality of AbsGS using 4.28M points on the reported comparisons.

Dense-benchmark robustness

Baselines that depend on global statistics or extensive recalibration run into severe overhead or OOM issues when scaled to Immersion v1.0.
